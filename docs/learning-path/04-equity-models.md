# Step 4: Equity models: how much should a share grow?

## Why it matters for us
This is where our values ("community over profit") meet the reality that people put real savings in and may need them back. The equity model decides what a member gets when they leave, and what the next member pays to join.

## The spectrum
| Model | What a leaving member gets | What a joining member pays | Feels like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market rate** | Whatever a buyer pays | Market price | Owning a condo |
| **Limited equity** | A formula: what they paid + an allowed annual increase + approved improvements | The same formula price | Ownership with a ceiling. Stays affordable. |
| **Zero / group equity** | Their deposit back | A small, fixed deposit | Collectively controlled renting |

## Designing a limited-equity formula
Typical ingredients (see [UHAB's guide](https://uhab.org/resource/guide-to-resale-policies/)):

- **Base:** what the member paid for their share.
- **Growth:** a fixed % per year (2 to 3% is common), or tied to inflation (CPI), sometimes with a cap.
- **Improvements:** credit for improvements the board approved, often depreciated over time.
- **Mortgage principal paydown:** does each member's share get credit as the co-op pays down its mortgage? In many limited-equity co-ops the co-op keeps it. This is a big lever, so decide deliberately.
- **Deductions:** unpaid charges, damage, and the transfer tax.
- **Transfer fee ("flip tax"):** a % of the sale that goes to the co-op's reserves.

Example: Chicago's La Villita co-op is reported to set share prices at $5,000, growing 3% a year compounded ([COLA Lab case study](https://eps.edu.miami.edu/_assets/pdf/chicago-field-note-new-generation-housing-cooperatives.pdf)). That's very low equity. Our numbers will likely be higher, because the members are funding the purchase themselves.

## A middle path many groups consider
- Founders who put in more cash get that extra back **as a loan with interest**, separately from their share. Then everyone holds an equal share that grows by the formula.
- Mortgage principal paydown can be shared: part credited to members, part kept by the co-op.

## Discuss
1. Fill in your [member snapshot](../worksheets/member-snapshot.md) first. How much does each of us care about our share growing?
2. If a founder leaves in 10 years, what would feel fair? Compute a few formula outcomes on $50,000 (e.g. 2%, 3%, CPI).
3. What should happen to mortgage principal paydown?
4. Would any of us be priced out by market-rate? Would any of us be hurt by a cap?
5. Do we want to be eligible for Chicago's limited-equity funding (with income limits)? Remember: market-rate co-ops aren't eligible.
