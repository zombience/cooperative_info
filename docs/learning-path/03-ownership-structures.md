# Step 3: Ownership structures

## Why it matters for us
This is the decision our lawyer will help us make. Our job is to understand the trade-offs well enough to say what we care about. As Supernuclear puts it, "the legal structure is an output from the inputs." Decide what you want first, then pick the form.

## The main options
| Structure | How it works | Good for us because... | Watch out for... |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Housing cooperative corporation** (in Illinois, usually under the [Not For Profit Corporation Act](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-805-business-organizations/il-st-sect-805-105-103-05/)) | The corporation owns the building; members own shares plus an occupancy right; one mortgage on the building. | Built for exactly this. Members can deduct their share of mortgage interest and property taxes (Section 216). Cook County homestead exemption. Eligible for limited-equity programs. | Formal: board, bylaws, annual filings. Few lenders make small co-op loans. Every share transfer is subject to Chicago transfer tax. |
| **LLC** (the Radish model) | An LLC owns the building; members own LLC interests; the operating agreement sets the rules. | Flexible, familiar to banks, easy to admit members with different capital. Real templates exist ([Dreamship](https://supernuclear.substack.com/p/dreamship-shares-their-co-ownership)). | No co-op tax treatment. Residents may effectively pay "rent" to themselves. Banks want personal guarantors. Exits aren't automatic. |
| **Tenancy in common (TIC)** | Each person owns a fractional share of the deed. | Simple and cheap to set up. | Everyone is usually on the mortgage, which hurts each person's borrowing capacity. A court can force a sale. Adding a member means changing the deed. A [Palm on Deakin](https://supernuclear.substack.com/p/case-study-the-palm-on-deakin) co-owner regretted it. |
| **Condo** | Each unit is separately deeded. | Individual mortgages, clear resale. | Converting a small building is costly. It's individual ownership, not much "co-op." |
| **Co-op + community land trust** | A land trust owns the land; the co-op owns the building on a long ground lease. | Permanent affordability, lower property tax, a backstop for lenders. | Less control. Chicago's land trust was restructured in 2025, so confirm what exists now. |
| **No-equity nonprofit** ([The Greenhouse](https://supernuclear.substack.com/p/how-the-greenhouse-set-up-its-non)) | A nonprofit owns the house; residents are members; friends lend the money. | Pure community-over-profit. | Members build no wealth. Lenders are friends. |

## Discuss
1. Answer Supernuclear's Big 3 for us: What return, if any, do we expect? How and when does money come back? Who makes major decisions?
2. How important is the property tax and income tax treatment of a true co-op to each of us?
3. Would we ever want non-resident investors? (That pushes toward an LLC.)
4. Do we want to be eligible for city limited-equity programs? (That pushes toward a limited-equity co-op.)

## Decide or record
