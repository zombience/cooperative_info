# Step 2: Case study: Radish, plus Chicago co-ops

## Why it matters for us
Radish is the group's original inspiration. It's a real, well-documented example of friends owning property together, including how it ended. Chicago co-ops show what's possible locally, under Illinois law and in this market.

## Key takeaways from Radish
- **Two hats.** Radish separated *owners* (who put in capital, in an LLC) from *residents* (who pay rent as a group). Owners got a target ~5% annual return.
- **Founders were repaid explicitly.** Startup costs were treated as a 0% loan repaid over 3 years. That's a useful pattern for our founders fronting money.
- **Exit was the weak spot.** There was no guaranteed way to cash out and no formula for pricing shares. After about 8 years, the families outgrew the property and it is being sold.
- **Tiered decisions.** Small reversible decisions are made by whoever does the work, spending decisions by vote, and new members by consensus.
- **It's an LLC, not a co-op corporation.** Residents pay rent to themselves and owe tax on it, and there's no co-op tax treatment.
