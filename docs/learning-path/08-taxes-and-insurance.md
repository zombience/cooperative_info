# Step 8: Taxes and insurance in Chicago

## Why it matters for us
Taxes shape both monthly costs and which structure makes sense. Chicago has three specific twists: high property taxes, a special homestead exemption process for co-ops, and a city transfer tax on every co-op share sale.

## Federal income tax: Section 216
If we form a true housing co-op that qualifies under [IRC § 216](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/216), each member can deduct their share of the co-op's mortgage interest and property taxes, much like a homeowner. To qualify, the co-op needs:

- **one class of stock**, and
- one of three tests: 80% of income from member-residents, 80% of floor space used by member-residents, or 90% of spending on the property.

**Watch:** renting units to non-members can break the 80% income test. That includes renting the third unit while waiting for the later household, or a permanently rented fourth unit in a 4-flat. An LLC (Radish model) doesn't get Section 216 treatment at all.

How members calculate it: [IRS Publication 530](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p530).

## Cook County property tax
- Co-op buildings get one tax bill (property class 2-13, assessed at 10% of market value).
- **Homeowner exemption:** each member-occupied unit can qualify. The co-op (not the member) files a [special paper form](https://prodassets.cookcountyassessoril.gov/s3fs-public/event/coopHomeownerExemption-2024_application_fillable_20250314.pdf) each year. Co-ops can't use the online system. [35 ILCS 200/15-175](https://ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003502000K15-175.htm) requires that each member's savings go to that member only.
- Ask the lawyer how senior and other exemptions flow through.
- **Appeal assessments.** It's free, but the windows are short: [Assessor](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/appeals), then the [Board of Review](https://www.cookcountyboardofreview.com/).
- Look up any property's history: [Assessor address search](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/address-search).

## Chicago transfer tax
- $5.25 per $500 of price ([City of Chicago](https://chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/revenue/tax_list/real_property_transfertax.html)).
- [Ruling #1](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/rev/supp_info/TaxRulingsandRegulations/RPTTRuling1.pdf): **every co-op share transfer is taxable, even a single share.** That applies when the later members buy in and when anyone leaves.

## Insurance
- **Master policy:** the building, plus liability. Ask about sewer backup and basement flooding (a common Chicago problem; see [CNT RainReady](https://cnt.org/)).
- **Directors and officers (D&O):** protects whoever serves on the board.
- **Each member:** an HO-6 style policy for contents and their own improvements ([NAHC](https://coophousing.org/living-in-a-housing-cooperative/)).
- Lenders will set minimum requirements.

## Discuss
1. How much is Section 216 treatment worth to each of us per year? (Rough estimate: your share of interest + taxes × your tax bracket. Itemizing only helps if it beats the standard deduction.)
2. How do we budget for transfer tax on each future buy-in or buyout?
3. Who files the homestead exemption every year? Put it on the calendar.
