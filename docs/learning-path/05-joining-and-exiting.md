# Step 5: Joining and leaving at different times

## Why it matters for us
**This is our defining challenge.** Two households are buying in now and a third plans to join in about two years. Over the decades, people will leave and others will join. A structure that only works if everyone arrives and stays together will fail us.

## Start with the worked example
Read **[Worked example: two households now, one later](../worksheets/scenario-staggered-entry.md)**. It walks through real-looking numbers for:

- the funding gap the later household leaves at closing,
- four ways to cover it (member loans, a deposit, founders buying the extra share, borrowing more),
- monthly costs while the third unit is empty or rented,
- a 3-flat vs. a 4-flat with one rented unit,
- what a departing household gets back.

## Joining later: things to settle
- **What does the later household pay?** Usually the formula price on the date it joins (Step 4), so the founding households aren't penalized for going first.
- **What does it commit to now?** A signed subscription agreement with a deposit gives everyone certainty. A handshake is cheaper but riskier.
- **Who covers its share of costs until it joins?** Founders, rental income, or reserves. Note the [tax caution about renting to non-members](../worksheets/scenario-staggered-entry.md#monthly-carrying-charges).
- **What if it doesn't join?** The co-op should be free to offer the membership to someone else.
- **Does it get a say before joining?** Consider involving the later couple in big decisions now, without votes, so they feel ownership of the choices they will inherit.

## Leaving: things to settle
- **Notice period:** for example 90 to 180 days.
- **Who buys the share?** An incoming member at the formula price; the co-op (right of first refusal); or the departing member finds a buyer the co-op approves (the [vacation-home LLC model](https://supernuclear.substack.com/p/how-to-co-buy-a-vacation-home-with-81c)).
- **What if nobody is ready to buy?** The co-op pays in installments over 12 to 36 months, or the departing member keeps paying carrying charges until the share sells. Write this down. It's where friendships get tested.
- **Cash-flow risk:** in a three-household co-op, each household is about a third of the income. One departure is a big deal. Keep a vacancy/buyout reserve alongside the building reserve.
- **Life events:** a couple separating (who keeps the membership?), death (can heirs keep the share?), job relocation, subletting while away.
- **Transfer tax:** Chicago taxes **every co-op share transfer** ([Ruling #1](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/rev/supp_info/TaxRulingsandRegulations/RPTTRuling1.pdf)). Decide who pays.

## Discuss
1. Which bridging option fits our members' cash right now?
2. Ask the later couple: what can they commit to now?
3. Role-play: one founder has to move for a job in year 3. Walk through exactly what happens, month by month, for the two remaining households.
4. How long can the co-op afford to carry an empty share?

## Decide or record
- How we cover the gap until the later household joins.
- Our exit rules (notice, who buys, payment timing, reserve). Take them to the lawyer to draft.
