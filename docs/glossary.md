# Glossary

Plain-language definitions of terms you'll run into. When a term has a Chicago or Illinois twist, it's noted. If you hit a word that isn't here, add it.

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Housing cooperative (co-op)** | A corporation that owns a building. Residents don't own their units; they own **shares** (or a membership) in the corporation, which gives them the right to live in a unit through an **occupancy agreement** or **proprietary lease**. |
| **Share / membership certificate** | The ownership stake a member buys. In most co-ops each household holds one membership tied to one unit. |
| **Occupancy agreement / proprietary lease** | The contract between the co-op and a member that grants the right to occupy a specific unit and sets the member's obligations (paying carrying charges, following house rules). |
| **Carrying charge** (or monthly assessment) | What each member pays the co-op every month. It covers the co-op's share of mortgage, property taxes, insurance, maintenance, utilities the co-op pays, and reserves. It is not rent, though it can feel like it. |
| **Blanket mortgage** | One mortgage on the whole building, owed by the co-op corporation, not by individual members. Its payment is part of everyone's carrying charge. |
| **Share loan** | A personal loan a member takes out to buy their co-op share, secured by the share. Common in big-city market-rate co-ops; harder to find for small or limited-equity co-ops. |
| **Market-rate co-op** | Members can sell their shares for whatever the market will pay. Wealth-building, but affordability isn't preserved. |
| **Limited-equity co-op (LEC / LEHC)** | Resale price of a share is capped by a formula written in the bylaws (for example: what you paid + a fixed % per year + credit for approved improvements). Keeps the home affordable for the next member. |
| **Zero-equity co-op** | Members pay a small, refundable membership fee; no appreciation. Closer to collective renting with control. |
| **Resale formula** | The written rule that sets what an exiting member gets back for their share in a limited-equity co-op. |
| **Buy-in** | What a new member pays to join (share price, sometimes plus fees). |
| **Buyout / redemption** | When a departing member's share is purchased, either by an incoming member or by the co-op itself. |
| **Right of first refusal (ROFR)** | The co-op's right to buy back (or approve the buyer of) a share before it goes to an outsider. |
| **Treasury share / unsold share** | A share the co-op owns because no member holds it yet. Relevant to us: the later household's share could sit as a treasury share until it joins. |
| **Member loan** | Money a member lends to the co-op (separate from buying a share), usually with a set interest rate and repayment schedule. A common way to have early members front money that later members effectively repay. |
| **Replacement reserve** | Savings held by the co-op for big future costs (roof, boiler, porches, tuckpointing). Sized by a **reserve study**. |
| **Operating reserve** | A cushion for short-term surprises, like a vacant unit or a late payment. Often 3 to 6 months of expenses. |
| **Reserve study** | A professional estimate of when major building components will need replacing and how much to save each year. |
| **LTV (loan-to-value)** | Mortgage amount divided by property value. A 75% LTV means members/the co-op put in 25% as equity. |
| **DSCR (debt service coverage ratio)** | What commercial lenders check: net operating income divided by annual mortgage payments. Co-op lenders often want 1.1x or higher. |
| **Section 216 (IRC § 216)** | The federal tax rule that lets co-op members deduct their share of the co-op's mortgage interest and property taxes, as long as the co-op meets the requirements (mainly that 80%+ of income comes from members). Confirm with a tax professional. |
| **Tenancy in common (TIC)** | A different structure: each owner holds a fractional deed share of the whole property. Simpler to set up, but financing, exits, and default by one owner are harder. |
| **LLC co-ownership** | The building is owned by an LLC, and members own LLC membership interests. Flexible, which is why many friend groups (including Radish) use it, but it is not a "housing cooperative" in the legal or tax sense. |
| **Community land trust (CLT)** | A nonprofit that owns land and leases it long-term to homeowners or co-ops, keeping homes permanently affordable. Chicago has a city-run CLT. |
| **Homestead exemption** | A property tax reduction for owner-occupants. In Illinois, co-op residents can qualify through the co-op; ask the lawyer how it's applied. |
| **Transfer tax** | Tax charged when real estate (and in Chicago, often a controlling interest in an entity that owns real estate) changes hands. Ask how it applies to share transfers. |
| **Bylaws** | The co-op's rulebook: membership, voting, board, share transfers, resale formula. Your lawyer drafts these with you. |
| **Subscription agreement** | The contract a new member signs to buy into the co-op. |
